Product bet — what to sell through this network

Working conclusion from the research conversation: the easiest repeat sales are not into Marriott/Ryman. They are into the 25–150 person contractors and local show vendors who keep getting called back to Opryland. Same buyer type, short cycles, referral-friendly.

Ranked by sell often + sell easily

  1. Job / crew / punch-list software (construction TI + FF&E) — Best fit. Beech, AZ Contracting, Tennessee Tile, Wolfe & Travis, Sunrise, SUNDEK, Walker-type shops already do repeat campus work. They care about crew scheduling across rooms/floors, punch lists / room-ready, permit & inspection status, receiving / install sequencing. Buyer is usually owner/PM, not IT. One win at Beech or AZ can refer across the Chase/Beech sub network. Highest volume + easiest close.
  2. Field service + recurring work-order software (maintenance / specialty trades) — Second easiest. Exterior cleaning, laundry routes (Five Star), landscape material (TerraScape), pool decks (SUNDEK), seasonal tent installs. Pain is dispatch + recurring jobs, not “enterprise CMMS.” Sell FSM lite: schedule → complete → invoice. Fast demos, monthly SaaS, sticky once adopted.
  3. Small-event labor / equipment ops tools (local EAC / indie AV) — Trade Show Tools, Nashville AV, Clarity, avad3, TLC floral. They compete under Encore/Freeman/GES and need labor call sheets, gear inventory / checkout, show-by-show CRM. Not another GSC portal — they already live in those. Sell the local operator stack. Very high intent, tiny companies, founder buys.
  4. Workforce scheduling + QA for outsourced hotel labor — Heart of the House (115 associates on-site) and similar hospitality staffing. Scheduling, call-offs, QA scores. One big account, harder sell than #1–3, but huge annual contract value if you land it. Fewer deals, bigger checks.

What not to chase first

  • Marriott/Gaylord IT (PMS, VMS, Boomer) — enterprise, incumbent, long procurement.
  • Milestone / Hanwha replacement — already deployed; Herring owns the relationship.
  • Freeman / GES / Encore “better portal” — they’re the platform; sell around them.
  • In-house horticulture ops — mostly staffed internally; few vendor seats.

Go-to-market shape

Treat Opryland as a referral hub, not the customer:

  1. Beachhead: Beech + AZ + Tennessee Tile + Nashville AV / Trade Show Tools.
  2. Story: “You’re always on campus — here’s how crews/punch/inventory stop leaking between jobs.”
  3. Expand: other Chase/Beech subs, then other Music Valley / hotel campus vendors.
  4. Later: Heart of the House / Five Star for bigger workforce deals.

If you only build one product first: construction + specialty-trade field ops (jobs, crews, punch, photos, simple scheduling). That wedge covers the densest HIGH / VERY HIGH SMB cluster in this database.

Three product bets defined

When we say “TI punch vs EAC labor vs FSM,” these are the three concrete product bets:

  1. TI punch — software for interior renovation crews tracking room punch lists and installs.
  2. EAC labor — software for local show vendors scheduling booth labor and gear.
  3. FSM — software for outdoor/service vendors dispatching recurring field jobs.

Glossary (acronyms used here)

TI — Tenant Improvement
Interior build-outs and renovations inside an existing building (retail, restaurants, ballrooms). Not new construction from dirt.
Punch / punch list
Leftover fix list at end of a job (paint, trim, fixtures). Tracked room-by-room until “room-ready.”
EAC — Exhibitor Appointed Contractor
Company an exhibitor hires to set up their booth, instead of (or in addition to) the show’s official contractor. Local AV/labor/scenic shops often work as EACs.
FSM — Field Service Management
Software for companies that send crews to job sites: schedule, dispatch, log work, invoice. Cleaning, laundry routes, landscape delivery, pool decks, exterior maintenance.
GC — General Contractor
Main builder who holds the overall contract and hires subcontractors (Chase, Beech, Hardaway).
Sub / subcontractor
Trade company hired by the GC (concrete, steel, tile, electrical, FF&E).
FF&E — Furniture, Fixtures & Equipment
Guest-room furniture, lamps, art, TVs, etc. AZ Contracting does this kind of install.
GSC — General Service Contractor
Official show contractor appointed by a trade show for freight, booth labor, carpet, furniture (Freeman, GES, Shepard, T3, Fern, RPMXPO, Alliance).
AV — Audio/Visual
Sound, screens, lighting, production (Encore in-house; Nashville AV / Clarity / avad3 as independents).
WFM — Workforce Management
Scheduling people, call-offs, hours, quality — what Heart of the House needs for housekeeping associates.
CMMS — Computerized Maintenance Management System
Heavier facility-maintenance software hotels use for building work orders. Different from lighter FSM sold to outside vendors.
VMS — Video Management System
Software that runs security cameras (Milestone at Opryland).
MEP — Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing
Engineering disciplines for building systems (I.C. Thomasson).
CFS — Cold-Formed Steel
Lightweight steel framing (Structural Evolution on the expansion).
AESS — Architecturally Exposed Structural Steel
Steel meant to be seen, not covered (Wylie on the Delta atrium).
ACV — Annual Contract Value
How much a customer pays per year. Bigger ACV usually means longer, harder sales.
SMB — Small/Medium Business
Roughly 10–200 employee companies — easier to sell than Marriott or Freeman.
Boomer / GOIT
Hotel exhibitor ordering portal (Boomer Commerce) and Gaylord Opryland IT. In-house electrical/internet for shows — not your primary buyer.

Path to finalize the product bet

Do not pick the product in a vacuum. Validate against real buyers in this network, then lock the wedge.

  1. Pick a 15-account outreach list — 8 TI/punch construction shops (Beech, AZ, Tennessee Tile, Wolfe & Travis, Sunrise, SUNDEK, Shaub, Southland), 4 EAC/AV/labor (Trade Show Tools, Nashville AV, Clarity, avad3), 3 FSM candidates (TerraScape, Five Star Laundry, exterior maintenance once employer verified).
  2. Run 10 short discovery calls — same script for all three bets: what tools do you use today (Excel, texts, Jobber, Buildertrend, nothing)? Where do jobs leak (missed punch, no-show labor, botched dispatch)? Who buys software (owner vs PM)? Budget range and last tool they paid for?
  3. Score each bet on four axes — (a) how often the pain shows up in calls, (b) how fast a founder/owner can say yes, (c) how many similar companies sit in the Opryland referral graph, (d) how thin a first product can be and still be useful.
  4. Choose one primary wedge — default bias from research: TI punch / construction field ops, unless discovery shows EAC labor or FSM pain is louder and more urgent.
  5. Write a one-page product thesis — who buys, first 5 features, first 5 design partners, price band, what you will not build in v1.
  6. Close 2–3 design partners from the beachhead list before writing serious code. Prefer companies already on campus repeatedly.
  7. Ship a narrow pilot — one workflow only (e.g. punch + photo + room-ready, or labor call sheet + checkout). Measure weekly usage for 4–6 weeks.
  8. Decide expand / pivot — if pilots use it without hand-holding, expand features and chase Chase/Beech referrals. If not, use call notes to pivot to the next-ranked bet without rebuilding the whole company list.

Decision gate: product bet is “final” when you have (1) a written thesis, (2) ≥2 design partners verbally committed, and (3) a 4–6 week pilot plan — not when the spreadsheet feels complete.
